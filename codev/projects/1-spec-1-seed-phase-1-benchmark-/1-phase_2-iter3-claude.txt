## Blocking: `convert_encyclicals` silently truncates 4 encyclicals mid-body

The iter-2 footnote-restart rule (`if num <= running_max: in_footnotes = True`, latched for the rest of the file) fires on the **first** non-monotonic number and then discards everything to EOF. In 4 documents that first anomaly is *inside the body*, so real prose is dropped with no error and no warning:

| document | code cuts at | true footnote start | body paragraphs lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mediator-dei` | idx 105 | idx 210 | **105** (of 210 — over half the encyclical) |
| `redemptoris-mater` | idx 71 | idx 164 | **93** |
| `ecclesiam-suam` | idx 99 | *none — no footnote block* | **85** |
| `dominum-et-vivificantem` | idx 131 | *none* | **64** |

≈347 body paragraphs. Two distinct anomaly classes are being conflated with a footnote block:

- **Typo'd paragraph numbers in body prose.** `mediator-dei` idx 105 declares `100` (sequence is …104, 105, **100**, 107, 108…) on the prose "These methods of participation in the Mass are to be approved and recommended…". `ecclesiam-suam` idx 99 declares `3` (should be 63) on "The fact that we are distinct from the world…", then continues 64…119 to the last paragraph of the document.
- **Section headings carrying a section number.** `redemptoris-mater` idx 71 = `{paragraph: 1, text: "The Church, the People of God present in all the nations of the earth"}`; `dominum-et-vivificantem` idx 131 = `{paragraph: 1, text: "Reason for the Jubilee of the Year 2000…"}`, idx 150 = `{paragraph: 3, …}`.

I checked the inverse direction too and it is nearly clean: only `ecclesia-de-eucharistia` keeps its footnote block (its footnotes carry `paragraph: null`, so ~100 land as `unnumbered para. N` prose entries; 42 match a strict `^\d+ (Cf.|Ibid|Code of Canon|…)` pattern). The other 55 restart-docs I spot-read (`arcanum-divinae`, `aeterni-patris`, `mens-nostra`, `providentissimus-deus`, `sacra-virginitas`, `ad-caeli-reginam`, `mystici-corporis-christi`, `spiritus-paraclitus`, `in-plurimis`, `mortalium-animos`, `diuturnum`, `octobri-mense`, `pacem-dei`, `principi-apostolorum`) cut correctly.

Three things make this worth blocking rather than noting:

1. **It is silent.** The converter discards half a document when its structural assumption is violated. Every other failure in this module raises `ConvertError` naming the file; this one returns a plausible-looking document. Whatever the cut rule becomes, it needs a guard that *fails* when the discarded tail doesn't look like bibliographic apparatus (e.g. contains long prose, or the numbering resumes above the pre-cut max) rather than trusting the heuristic.
2. **The test locks the wrong number in.** `test_convert_encyclicals_real` asserts `10263`, and `test_convert_encyclicals_citations_bind_to_declared_numbers` happens to probe `Ad Petri Cathedram` and `Aeterni Patris` — both correct cases. Per-document citation uniqueness is again satisfied *by* the truncation, which is the same verification-by-aggregate-property trap as iter-1's "unique by construction" positional fix. A test naming these 4 documents (e.g. `Mediator Dei, para. 210` exists; `Ecclesiam Suam, para. 119` exists) would have caught it.
3. **Downstream validity, not just coverage.** Phase 5's overlap exclusion ("a wrong-work `verify_source` negative whose text also occurs in the attributed work") is evaluated against the standardized corpus. Text missing from the corpus makes that exclusion silently fail, producing negatives whose declared ground truth is wrong — in a benchmark whose headline number is hallucination/provenance fidelity.

One consequence to plan for: once the body is retained, **genuine duplicate declared numbers appear** and `_require_unique_citations` will (correctly) reject — `mediator-dei` para 100 twice (idx 99, 105), `ecclesiam-suam` para 3 twice (idx 2, 99), `redemptoris-mater` paras 1 and 2 twice, `dominum-et-vivificantem` paras 1 and 3 twice. So the fix needs a citation rule for a typo'd/section-heading duplicate (the existing non-canonical `unnumbered para.` marker seems like the natural home). For calibration, I tried the obvious tightening — "cut at the earliest index whose declared numbers strictly ascend to EOF *and* restart below the preceding max" — and it recovers `mediator-dei`, `redemptoris-mater`, `dominum-et-vivificantem` and keeps `arcanum-divinae` correct, but still truncates `ecclesiam-suam` (its typo'd `3` is followed by an ascending run to EOF). So a purely numeric single-signal rule looks unlikely to close this; text length/shape of the discarded block is probably the discriminating signal. I'd validate any candidate rule per-document across all 201 files, not by a corpus total.
