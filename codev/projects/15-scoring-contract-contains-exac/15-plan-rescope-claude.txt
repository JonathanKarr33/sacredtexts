## What's good

## The blocking problem: Phase 3 is far larger than the plan thinks

The plan frames the refused rebaseline as a peer of Phase 1's "+20, +1". It isn't. Scanning the committed answers with a *leading-sentence-only* decline pattern:

| run | model | n | declines | acc now | acc w/ refusals excluded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| first-run | claude-haiku | 100 | ~55 | 25% | **56%** |
| first-run | llama-3.1-8b | 100 | ~17 | 1% | 1% |
| tutorial | haiku | 100 | ~34 | 47% | **71%** |
| tutorial | haiku-search | 100 | ~35 | 52% | **80%** |
| tutorial | opus | 100 | ~34 | 66% | **100%** |
| tutorial | opus-search | 100 | ~25 | 74% | **99%** |

Three consequences the plan does not address:

**1. It re-inverts the sign it exists to fix.** The spec's problem statement is "the headline inverts the sign of search gains" (haiku 47% vs haiku-search 40% → 52% under containment, a 5-point gain). Exclude refusals and **opus 100% ranks above opus-search 99%** — search-on looks worse again, and "opus scores 100%" is a false headline for a model that declined a third of the benchmark. Decision #2 is baked and I'm not challenging it; the fix is to implement it honestly: the summary and the leaderboard must carry an **answer rate / coverage** figure `(correct+incorrect)/n` adjacent to every accuracy, and the ranking semantics must be documented. Phase 4 says "refused count/rate per model," which is in the right direction, but the plan treats it as a viewer nicety rather than the thing that keeps accuracy from lying. Phase 3 should emit coverage in `summary.json` and `summary.md`, not just the three raw counts.

**2. Phase 5's prose instructions are written against figures that will have moved by ~30 points.** Phase 5 says "rebaselined figures" and "do NOT alter the correct 8 of 34 / 24 of 33 contains counts" — fine — but the *narrative* (search gain, the tutorial's headline story) changes direction. Worth surfacing the Phase-3 numbers to the architect before Phase 5 writes prose.

**3. A real conservativeness trap exists in the evidence.** Two `opus-search` cells currently score **correct** while opening with a decline ("I couldn't confirm… / my searches didn't bring up…") and then delivering the text. A matcher that fires on the opening sentence would convert two correct answers into refusals. The plan's test list covers "wrapped exact" and "near-miss" negatives but not **"decline-prefixed answer that nevertheless delivers the reference."** That case should be a named, pre-registered unit test — and it argues for a shape rule like *decline phrase AND no `contains_reference` hit* rather than phrase-matching alone.

## Unresolved contract decision that breaks an existing guard

Phase 3 says refusal "short-circuits grading" and the plan is silent on whether a refused cell still emits `exact` / `normalized` / `token_f1` / `contains_reference`. This is not cosmetic:

- `test_contains_reference_rebaseline.py::test_similarity_projection_unchanged` hashes the `(exact, normalized, token_f1)` projection across **all** cells against a SHA frozen from the *pre-containment* committed scores. Drop those keys on ~130 refused cells and the hash breaks. The plan's answer — "capture a **fresh** pre-change projection oracle (now over the containment-era committed scores)" — **discards the phase-1 anchor** and makes the witness chain self-referential.
- Keep emitting the submetrics (let `outcome` decide the *headline*, not the metric fields) and the existing oracle survives untouched, as do `test_contains_reference_invariants`' two invariants (a refusal has `normalized == contains_reference == 0`, so `correct == normalized` still holds). That is a strictly stronger evidence position for one line of design. I'd make it the plan's stated decision.
- Either way, `_SUBMETRICS` denominators need a ruling: `score.py:372` computes `acc[sm]/acc["n"]`. If refusals stay in `n`, every submetric average gets diluted by cells that were never graded. Pick one and pin it.
- `EXPECTED_ACCURACY` (0.52/0.47/0.74/0.66) and `test_first_run_overall_and_lds_tradition_pinned` (0.25, 0.76) will all move. The plan says "extend the oracle" but doesn't flag that these pins change.

## Phase 2: the headline rule under a grader subset is undefined

The plan says "headline stays `normalized OR contains_reference`" — for the default set. But `--evaluator strict` is explicitly selectable, and nothing states what `correct` means then. Worse, there's a concrete latent bug: `score.py:334` seeds the accumulator with `"contains_reference": 0` and `summary` iterates the static `_SUBMETRICS`, so `score --evaluator strict` would emit **`contains_reference: 0.0`** into `summary.json` — a wrong number, not an absent one. Phase 2 needs (a) a stated headline rule under subsets (e.g. OR over selected graders' verdicts) and (b) `_SUBMETRICS` derived from the selected grader set rather than a module constant.

## Phase 4: file list is incomplete

The named files exist, but the ones that actually hold the correct/incorrect taxonomy are missing:

- `apps/viewer/src/lib/explorer.ts` — `FailureMode`, `ExplorerFilters.correct` (binary `"correct" | "incorrect"`), `filterQuestions`, `questionStats`. This is the primary edit site and isn't named (the plan says "explorer lib").
- `apps/viewer/src/components/ExplorerFilters.tsx` and `routes/ExplorerPage.tsx` — the URL contract is `?task=&tradition=&correct=&mode=`; adding `refused` changes it.
- `routes/StandingsPage.tsx` / `lib/aggregate.ts` — standings ranks on `overall?.accuracy` straight from `summary.json`, so the denominator change silently moves cross-run standings *values*. The plan's "no standings-key drift" covers keys, not values.
- **Four display categories, not three.** Missing roster cells already have `correct: false` and render as `mode=missing`; the plan never says how `missing` and `refused` coexist.
- **Search-on/off pairing mechanism is unspecified.** The only signal is the `[web_search]` / `[no-tools]` marker inside `summary.panel[id].model` (the viewer's `PanelEntry` has no `api_model` field). Pairing means stripping that marker to find the base model. Also: `panel` is only emitted when a run config was supplied, and **first-run has no on/off pair at all** — so the contrast affordance needs a no-pairs fallback, and the acceptance criterion "a test fails if the contrast is absent" needs to not fire on first-run.
