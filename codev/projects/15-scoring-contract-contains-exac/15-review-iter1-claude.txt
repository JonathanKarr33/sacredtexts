### What's strong

**The scoring logic is well-built and genuinely spec-compliant.** I traced the six decisions through the code rather than trusting the PR body:

- **`outcomes.classify_refusal`** is honestly conservative. The two-part rule (decline cue inside `LEADING_WINDOW` normalized chars **AND** reference-not-delivered) is correct, and the `LEADING_WINDOW + 64` slice margin is sound (longest normalized cue ≈ 23 chars, so a cue starting just inside the window is still found). The comment at `outcomes.py:117-121` explaining why the containment half is **not** redundant — under `--evaluator strict` a contained-but-not-normalized answer scores `correct=0`, so only that check stops a decline-prefixed correct retrieval being mislabelled — is exactly the kind of load-bearing reasoning that prevents a future "simplification" from reintroducing a bug.
